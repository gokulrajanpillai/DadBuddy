      If you think something is seriously wrong

      Trust your instincts — you know your baby best. This app only records what you log. It cannot diagnose anything and is not a medical device.

      Call 999 Call 111 111 online
      Call 999 or go to A&E if your baby…
      • has difficulty breathing, is breathing very fast, grunting, or sucking in under their ribs
      • has blue, grey, pale or blotchy skin, lips or tongue
      • is floppy, very hard to wake, or cannot be woken
      • has a rash that does not fade when you press a glass against it
      • has a fit (seizure)
      • has a temperature of 38°C or higher and is under 3 months old
      • has a temperature of 39°C or higher and is 3 to 6 months old (if they had vaccines in the last 2 days, call 111)
      • has a temperature under 36°C (re-check twice within 10 minutes first), or feels cold, clammy or shivery
      • has a weak, high-pitched or continuous cry
      • has not had a wet nappy in the last 12 hours
      • is not feeding
      • has swollen lips, tongue or throat

      Source: NHS — when to get urgent medical help for babies and children under 5

      Temperature and fever
      • Use a digital thermometer under the arm. Hold their arm against their side for as long as the thermometer says (usually about 15 seconds). Forehead strips and ear thermometers are less accurate for babies.
      • A normal temperature is about 36.4°C. A high temperature is 38°C or more.
      • Under 3 months, 38°C or more: get medical help straight away. (NHS urgent-help page: 999 or A&E. NHS fever page: 111.)
      • 3 to 6 months, 39°C or more: get medical help straight away, as above.
      • Any age: call 111 if a high temperature lasts 5 days or more, or comes with other worrying signs.
      • Do not give paracetamol under 2 months, or ibuprofen under 3 months or under 5kg, unless a doctor tells you to.
      • Keep offering feeds. Do not sponge them down or wrap them up.

      Sources: NHS — fever in children · NHS — how to take your baby's temperature

      Feeding and "Today's checks"
      • Feed responsively. Offer a feed when your baby shows early hunger cues: stirring, rooting, sucking their fist, murmuring. Crying is a late sign. You cannot overfeed a breastfed baby.
      • In the first few weeks most babies feed at least 8 to 12 times in 24 hours, sometimes more, and every 1 to 3 hours in the first few days. The app compares feeds in the last 24 hours against this rough guide while your baby is under 6 weeks.
      • Bottle feeding: feed when they seem hungry, take your time, and do not worry if they don't finish the bottle. Never force them to finish.
      • A check only shows "Below guide" once you've been logging for its whole time window, so starting the app halfway through a day won't set it off.
      • Breastfeeding worries? Ask early. Talk to your midwife or health visitor, or call the National Breastfeeding Helpline on 0300 100 0212.

      Sources: NHS — breastfeeding: the first few days · NHS — bottle feeding advice

      Safe sleep
      • Always put your baby down on their back, with their feet at the foot of the cot.
      • Use a separate cot or Moses basket with a firm, flat mattress, in the same room as you for every sleep for at least the first 6 months.
      • Keep the cot clear: no pillows, duvets, bumpers, pods, nests or soft toys.
      • Keep the room at about 16 to 20°C. Use a lightweight blanket or a baby sleeping bag, and never a hot water bottle or electric blanket.
      • Never sleep with your baby on a sofa or armchair. Don't share a bed if anyone in it smokes, has had alcohol or drugs, or takes medicine that makes them drowsy, or if your baby was premature or small at birth.
      • Newborns sleep about 18 hours in 24, in short stretches. Every baby has their own pattern.

      Sources: NHS — reduce the risk of SIDS · NHS — helping your baby to sleep

      Making up formula safely
      • Make up one feed at a time, when it's needed.
      • Use water that was boiled and left for no more than 30 minutes, so it's still at least 70°C. Don't add extra powder.
      • Cool the feed to body temperature. Never warm it in a microwave.
      • Throw away any milk left in the bottle after a feed.
      • If you need to take a feed out: keep it in the fridge and use within 24 hours, within 4 hours in a cool bag with an ice pack, or within 2 hours at room temperature.

      Sources: NHS — making up baby formula · NHS — formula milk questions

      Vitamin D
      • Breastfed babies should have a daily vitamin D supplement of 8.5 to 10 micrograms from birth, even if the mother takes one too.
      • Babies having more than 500ml of formula a day don't need it, because formula is already fortified. The app stops the check when logged formula is over 500ml in 24 hours.
      • Don't give two supplements together (for example vitamin drops and cod liver oil). Families on Healthy Start can get free vitamin drops.

      Source: NHS — vitamins for children

      Mum's medicines while breastfeeding
      • Tell your GP, midwife, health visitor, pharmacist or dentist that you're breastfeeding whenever you talk about medicines.
      • Small amounts of a medicine can pass into breast milk, but usually very little, and very few medicines are unsafe while breastfeeding.
      • Paracetamol is usually the best painkiller to take while breastfeeding. Check first if it's combined with other medicines.
      • If a professional thinks you need an anti-inflammatory painkiller, ibuprofen is thought to be the safest choice. Check with a pharmacist or doctor first.
      • Not recommended while breastfeeding: codeine, decongestant tablets, liquids or powders, aspirin for pain relief, and herbal remedies. Ask before using decongestant sprays or antihistamines.
      • Don't stop a prescribed medicine without talking to your GP.
      • The doses and gaps in DadBuddy are what you enter. Always follow the prescription or the leaflet. If you think too much has been taken, call 111 for advice, or 999 if someone is unwell.

      Sources: NHS — breastfeeding and medicines · NHS — paracetamol for adults · NHS — ibuprofen for adults
